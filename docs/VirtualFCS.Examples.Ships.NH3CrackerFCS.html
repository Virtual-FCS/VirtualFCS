Inputs and input-side interfaces

This is a self-contained example with no external signal inputs or physical connectors. The electrical load is prescribed internally: resistance decreases linearly from 0.1 to 0.005 ohm between 20 and 40 s, increasing fuel-cell demand. Component-level settings include a total vaporizer heat input of 70 kW, a 5 m3 hydrogen buffer tank, and a PSA separation efficiency of 0.9. Like the cooler, the vaporizer uses one dynamic fluid node with modelStructure = av_b. A single prescribed heat source supplies the full 70 kW through heatPorts[1]. The pipe geometry and total fluid volume are unchanged. Dynamic mass storage is retained, so inlet and outlet mass flows can differ while the fluid inventory changes.

2. Model description

This example represents an ammonia-to-electricity system for ship power-system studies. It couples ammonia storage and feed conditioning, catalytic ammonia decomposition, gas cooling, simplified pressure-swing adsorption (PSA), hydrogen buffering, and a hydrogen fuel-cell system supplying a variable resistive load.

Ammonia-rich gas is drawn from the storage tank by a controlled pump, heated in the vaporizer pipe, and passed through a check valve into the segmented AmmoniaCrackerDyn. A distributed electric heater supplies heat to the endothermic cracking process. The cracked gas is cooled before entering PSASimple; the separated hydrogen enters the fuel-cell system's buffer tank, while the tail stream is discharged to a fixed-pressure boundary.

The ammonia feed PI controller compares a ramped hydrogen tank pressure target with a filtered pressure measurement. Its bounded output passes through a first-order filter before commanding the pump. A separate PI controller removes heat from the cooler to approach the PSA inlet temperature target; it cannot add heat. The fuel-cell assembly includes hydrogen pressure reduction, recirculation and purge, cathode air supply, cooling, and a battery supplying balance-of-plant equipment.

The cracker heater uses feed-forward plus a cascade. The feed-forward term Q_heater_ff supplies the reaction heat expected at the commanded NH3 feed. The inner PI controller trims this to hold the mean bed temperature, which responds quickly to heater changes. A slow outer PI controller shifts the bed-mean set-point so that the outlet temperature reaches T_out_target. Controlling the outlet temperature directly causes a limit cycle at low feed: when the heat supply is short, a cold reaction zone moves through the bed and reaches the outlet only after 150-200 s.

The model is intended to explore coupled process and electrical transients, controller interactions, and the effect of load changes on hydrogen inventory. The media and separation models are simplified system-level representations. In particular, the vaporizer uses the cracked-ammonia gas medium rather than an explicit liquid-to-vapor phase-change model. Initial conditions describe a preheated process, not a complete cold-start sequence.

PI_heater: u_s = T_out_target + outerPI.y, u_m = mean(ammoniaCracker.T), u_ff = Q_heater_ff

outerPI:   u_s = T_out_target, u_m = temp_out_cracker.T

The hydrogen reducer is sized at its nominal flow using the difference between the initial buffer-tank pressure and the anode pressure set-point. Net electrical power subtracts the distributed cracker heater demand from the fuel-cell system power, which already accounts for its balance-of-plant consumption. The separate vaporizer heat source and ammonia-pump power supply are not deducted in this expression.

See AmmoniaCrackerDyn, ElectricHeatingDistr, PSASimple, and FuelCellSystem for the component formulations.
